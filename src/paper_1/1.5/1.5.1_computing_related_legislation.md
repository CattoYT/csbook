## The Data Protection Act 1998
The Data Protection Act is responsible for regulating how much data companies can keep about you. 

### Requirements
The law states that organisations must contect the government and declare what data they collect about their users.  
They must also:
- Ensure security around the data
- Only collect necessary data required for their activities
- Have a specified Data Controller responsible for the organisation's compliance

### The 8 principles of the DPR
- The data must be lawful, fair, and transparent.
- Personal data must be collected only for specific, explicit, and legitimate purposes
- Collected data must be limited to only what is necessary
- Personal data must not be kept longer than necessary
- Personal data must be handled securely
- Data Controllers must document their compliance with the DPA
- The user has the right to delete or modify their own personal data
- Personal data must not be transferred to countries without adequate data protection

## The Computer Misuse Act 1990
The CMA is the act responsible for preventing unauthorised access, modification, and deletion of computer systems or data. 

The law prevents:
- Unauthorised access to systems
- Planting of malware to impair a system
- Attempting to obtain unauthorised access to system
- Deliberate destruction of data
- Theft of data
- Piracy
- Fraud

### Preventing unauthorised access
The first main part of the law is to prevent the intentional unauthorised accesss to computer material.


### Unauthorised access ith the intent to commit or facilitate further crimes
This part of the act applies to those who have unauthorised access to a system, and then have the intent to use that access to commit further crimes.

### Unauthorised access with intent to impair a system
This is an escalated version of the previous part, where you have the intent to do something with the aquired data, particularly altering or damaging systems.

## The Copyright and Patents Act 1988
This act is responsible for ensurong that property owned either by a company or individual can be protected from modification, plagiarism, etc.  
This targets digital files, images, and software. While it doesn't prevent the actual copying of the file, it allows the owner to take legal action.

## The Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act 2000
This act allows the government to monitor and record internet activity to prevent cybercrime.  
The law has a VERY large scope, allowing for investigatory agencies to:
- Intercept communications
- Aquire and disclose data relating to communications
- The ability to request data from service providers
- Carrying out varying methods of surveillance
- Use covert human intelligence sources to aquire additional information
- A full backdoor in all encrypted systems, allowing the government to access any encrypted data

The law still mandates that these scoped activities must be done under strict legal authorisation, and that the government must have a valid reason to carry out these activities.  
They must still abide by the Human Rights Act (ensuring lawful and respectful treatment), particularly the right to privacy.

The law allows the state to:
- Issue an interception warrant to investigate under the premise of preventing/detecting crime or ensuring the integrity of the UK
- Hide obtained information from being revealed in court if not needed
- Allows police, government organisations, etc to demand providers/comapnies to hand over any retained information of users
- Demand the unlocking of encrypted information (phone passcodes, encryption keys, etc. This becomes a criminal offence if they do not oblige)
- Force ISPs to have necessary surveillance equipment
- Force ISPs to provide secret access to customer activity

### Covert surveillance types
The law regulates 5 categories of covert surveillance:
- Directed surveillance 
- Intrusive surveillance (keyloggers, bugs, etc)
- Covert human intelligence sources (Spies, undercover agents)
- Accessing communication data (Subpoenas, warrants, etc)
- Intercepting communication data (MITMs, etc)

###  2016 Amendment
The 2016 amendment made a few changes to prevent abuse:
- The warrant mist ne signed by the secretary of state and a judge
- The crime suspected must have a 6 month minimum sentence
- Providers must log 12 months of internet connection records
- The law is overseen under the Investigatory Powers Commissioners
